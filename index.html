TMDB¶

TMDB's observation unit is single movie.

It includes following variables:

title, original_title, release_date, runtime,

budget, revenue,

vote_average, vote_count, popularity,

genres, keywords, overview, tagline, homepage, id,

original_language, spoken_languages

production_companies, production_countries, status

For TMDB, we can see it contains the basic information of multiple aspects about a movie.

Q1: What is most relevant with the rating of a film?

Does a higher budget means a higher rating? Does a higher rating relates to a higher revenue? Is there a specific month that more movies are released? Like summer?

We'll answer these questions with TMDB dataset. Correlation Matrix is considered to visualize the answer for major question. For minor questions, linear regretion is considered to be used.

The hypothesis is that: higher budget might not directly related to a higher rating, but a relatively high correlation coefficient is expected. Also a high correlation coefficient for revenue. Though there might be movies that are highly rated by a very small crowd, causing high average rating, but little revenue. Summer and winter breaks are expected to have more movies released for school holiday reason.

Q2: Is there a relable director or actor that consistently produces high rating movies?

Is there a director or an actor who always receives high ratings? Does a director with more likes in facebook also produce high rating movies? Is facebook rating related to the average score of movies?

We'll answer these questions with IMDB dataset. We are going to look at the correlation coefficient between certain individuals' facebook likes and the average ratins of the movies they are involved. High correlation coefficient is expected.

In [8]:
def parse_json_names(json_str, max_items=None):
    try:
        items = json.loads(json_str)

        names = []
        for item in items:
          names.append(item['name'])

        if max_items:
          return names[:max_items]
        else:
          return names

    except:
        return []

Now we apply parse_json_names to genres, keywords, production_companies, production_countries, and spoken_languages.

Q1: What is most relevant with the rating of a film?

Does a higher budget means a higher rating? Does a higher rating relates to a higher revenue? Is there a specific month that more movies are released? Like summer?

We shall use a heatmap to visualize the correlation coefficients between the quantitative variables, so we can see not only the correlation between rating and other variables, but discover more correlations fro exploratory purpose.

In [19]:

             'runtime', 'popularity',

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

We spot that the highest correlation coeffitient appears between revenue and profit. Vote count has several relatively strong correlations with revenue, profit and popularity, which makes sense. Increasing budget might also increase revenue, but interestingly, have a negative correlation coefficient with rating.

Rating doesn't necessarily correlate with any other quatitative variables.
